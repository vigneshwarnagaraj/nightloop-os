Learn Air Traffic Control with NIGHTLOOP

A practical course from your first taxi clearance to running a whole airport. Read a chapter, then try the matching lesson in the simulator.

How to use this guide

Each chapter explains one part of ATC in plain words, shows what controllers and pilots actually say, and ends with how to do it in NIGHTLOOP. The lessons at the end build up step by step.

Start with one position. At the top of NIGHTLOOP, under You work, switch on only GND (Ground). The AI controllers handle everything else while you learn. Add TWR, APP and CTR one at a time.
NIGHTLOOP is a training and entertainment simulator. Real ATC follows national rules (FAA, ICAO, CAA) that are more detailed than anything here. Never use this for real-world aviation.

ATC in one page

Air traffic control keeps aircraft apart and traffic flowing. The sky around an airport is divided into areas, and each area belongs to one controller ("position"). As a flight moves, it is handed off from one controller to the next.

PositionWhereMain jobs
Clearance Deliveryat the gateGives the route, first altitude and squawk code before departure.
Groundaprons and taxiwaysPush back, taxi routes, runway crossings. Never on an active runway without Tower's OK.
Towerrunways and about 5-10 NM aroundLine up, takeoff and landing clearances, spacing on the runway, go-arounds.
Approach / Departureabout 10-40 NM (radar)Climbs departures out, brings arrivals down and lines them up on the final approach (vectors, ILS).
Centeren-route, hundreds of NMHigh-level traffic between airports, climbs, descents, direct routings, handoffs.

Life of a flight

Gate→Ground: push back, taxi→Tower: line up, takeoff→Departure / Center: climb, cruise→Center: descend→Approach: vectors, ILS→Tower: cleared to land→Ground: taxi to gate

In NIGHTLOOP you see exactly this: departures call Ground from a gate, Tower when holding short, then climb away; arrivals call Center, then Approach at 40 NM, Tower at 6 NM final, and Ground after leaving the runway.

Talking on the radio

Radio time is precious, so messages follow a fixed order: who you call, who you are, the message. Controllers start with the callsign.

ATC: United 4527, turn left heading two seven zero, descend and maintain five thousand.
Pilot: Left heading two seven zero, down to five thousand, United 4527.
  • Readback: pilots repeat every instruction about headings, altitudes, speeds, runways, hold-short and clearances. The controller must check the readback is correct.
  • Callsign: airline radio name + flight number ("Speedbird 123" = British Airways 123, "Lufthansa", "American", "Delta"...). NIGHTLOOP shows the ICAO code (BAW123) on the radar but speaks the radio name.
  • Keep it short: one or two instructions per call is normal; three is the most.
  • "Say again" = repeat. "Roger" = received. "Wilco" = will comply. "Affirm" = yes. "Negative" = no. "Standby" = wait.

Runway numbers are the magnetic heading divided by ten. Parallel runways get L, C, R (left, center, right). Each runway has two ends: 10L in one direction is 28R in the other.

Separation rules

Separation is the minimum distance between two aircraft. If either the horizontal or the vertical gap is big enough, they are separated.

WhereHorizontalVertical
Approach / Tower radar (near the airport)3 NM1,000 ft
Center (en-route radar)5 NM1,000 ft (2,000 ft above FL410)
Same runwayOnly one aircraft on the runway at a time (landing aircraft must have the runway free before it crosses the threshold).

NIGHTLOOP's red conflict bar warns when two AI planes come closer than 3 NM and 1,000 ft, or will within a minute. It explains the conflict and suggests a fix (Fix it button).

Ways to fix a conflict, in order of preference: altitude (climb one, descend the other), heading (turn one 20-30° away), speed (slow the one behind). The eastbound/westbound rule helps en-route: eastbound flights fly odd thousands (FL330, 350, 370), westbound even (FL340, 360, 380).

Wake turbulence

Big aircraft leave strong whirlwinds behind them. A small plane behind a heavy one needs extra space: about 4-6 NM on final, or 2-3 minutes between departures on the same runway. NIGHTLOOP's AI Tower waits 75 s between departures and the AI Approach keeps about 5 NM between arrivals.

Clearance Delivery, squawk, frequencies and ATIS

Before an airliner moves, it needs its IFR clearance: permission to fly its filed route, with the first altitude, the departure frequency and a squawk code (a 4-digit transponder code that identifies it on radar; digits 0-7 only).

The easy way to remember the parts is C-R-A-F-T: Clearance limit (destination), Route, Altitude (initial, and the expected cruise level), Frequency (departure), Transponder (squawk).

Pilot: Clearance, JetBlue 4513, gate Mike Two Seven, IFR to Kennedy, information Bravo, request clearance.
DEL: JetBlue 4513, cleared to Kennedy airport as filed, climb and maintain five thousand, expect flight level three five zero one zero minutes after departure, departure frequency one two five decimal four, squawk four five two one.
Pilot: Cleared to Kennedy as filed, five thousand, expect flight level three five zero, one two five decimal four, squawk four five two one, JetBlue 4513.
  • ATIS is a recorded weather and runway broadcast. Each new version gets a letter (Alpha, Bravo...). Pilots say "information Bravo" to show they have it. NIGHTLOOP builds the ATIS from the real weather report (METAR) of your airport - see the weather box at the top (wind, altimeter); hover it for the frequencies.
  • Frequencies are the real ones of the airport where known (Clearance, Ground, Tower, Departure, Approach), e.g. "contact Tower one one eight decimal three".
  • Altimeter (USA, inches: "altimeter two niner niner two") or QNH (elsewhere, hectopascals: "QNH one zero one three") lets pilots set the correct height.

In NIGHTLOOP

Switch on DEL under "You work". Departures call "request clearance" from the gate (Ground window list shows Clearance). Press it to give the standard clearance, or on the plane card fill in initial altitude, cruise level and squawk. Typed: CLR or CLR A050 FL350 SQ4521. After the clearance the pilot calls Ground. When DEL is AI, the AI gives the clearance and you hear it in the Ground window.

The full takeoff clearance

TWR: JetBlue 4513, wind two seven zero at one zero, runway two eight right, cleared for takeoff.
Pilot: Cleared for takeoff two eight right, JetBlue 4513.
TWR: JetBlue 4513, contact Chicago Departure one two five decimal four, good day.

In NIGHTLOOP the wind is added automatically from the live weather. After takeoff give HO to send the plane to Departure (Tower's handoff); if you forget, it switches by itself after a minute.

Ground control

Ground controls everything moving on the taxiways and aprons. Ground never lets a plane onto a runway: crossing a runway needs a clear "cross runway ..." instruction (from Tower in the real world).

Typical exchange

Pilot: Ground, Cathay 3522, gate F10, ready to push and start.
GND: Cathay 3522, push back approved.
Pilot: Push back complete, ready to taxi.
GND: Cathay 3522, runway one zero left, taxi via Bravo, Charlie One, Alpha, hold short runway one zero center.
Pilot: (stops before 10C) holding short runway one zero center.
GND: Cathay 3522, cross runway one zero center.

In NIGHTLOOP

  1. Open the Ground window (house icon). Planes waiting for you are listed on the right.
  2. Push back button (or type PB), then Taxi (TAXI uses the active runway).
  3. To choose the route yourself: Route → click the yellow taxiway names in order → Send taxi. Or type TAXI 10L VIA B C1 A.
  4. Planes stop 75 m before runways they must cross; press Cross (CROSS) when no one is landing or taking off there.
  5. HOLD stops a taxiing plane (traffic conflict), CONT continues.
  6. Arrivals call "clear of runway, request taxi to gate": Taxi to gate (TAXI or TAXI C17).
Look ahead: before crossing, check the Tower list for arrivals within 4 NM of that runway.

Tower

Tower owns the runways. Its two key decisions: who may use the runway now, and is it safe.

Pilot: Tower, Speedbird 123, holding short runway one zero left, ready for departure.
TWR: Speedbird 123, runway one zero left, line up and wait.
TWR: Speedbird 123, runway one zero left, cleared for takeoff.
...
Pilot: Tower, American 55, six miles final runway one zero left.
TWR: American 55, runway one zero left, cleared to land.
  • Never clear a takeoff if an arrival is closer than about 3-4 NM, or the runway is occupied.
  • Give the landing clearance by 2 NM final. A plane without clearance goes around (climbs away and comes back).
  • Go-around: GA - use it when the runway will not be free in time.

In NIGHTLOOP

Tower window: departures waiting and arrivals on approach are listed with buttons. Commands: LUAW (line up and wait), CTO (cleared for takeoff, optionally CTO H250 A050 for a heading and altitude after takeoff), CL (cleared to land), GA (go around). The runway status line tells you which runway is occupied.

Approach

Approach turns a stream of arrivals from all directions into a neat line on the final approach, about 5 NM apart, ending on the ILS (a radio beam that guides the plane to the runway).

The classic pattern

Downwind (flying opposite to the landing direction, beside the airport) → base (turn 90°) → intercept (about 30° to the final course) → established on the localizer → descend on the glide slope.

APP: Delta 12, turn left heading one two zero, descend and maintain three thousand.
APP: Delta 12, turn right heading zero seven zero, maintain three thousand until established, cleared ILS runway one zero left approach.
APP: Delta 12, contact Tower.
  • Intercept angle: 30° or less, at least 2 NM before the final approach fix (about 8-10 NM from the runway).
  • Typical altitudes: 3,000 ft above the airport at 10 NM; speed 210 kt on downwind, 180 on base, 170 to the final fix.
  • Spacing: use speed first (S170), then a wider turn, then a 360 if needed.

In NIGHTLOOP

Switch on APP under "You work". Arrivals call at 40 NM and hold at 18 NM if you ignore them. Give headings (L120), altitudes (A030), then ILS 10L (they intercept by themselves when the angle is 30° or less). The Approach window shows the 40 NM zone, range rings and dashed extended centerlines of the active runways.

Center

Center works high-altitude traffic over large areas: climbing departures to cruise, keeping crossing traffic apart, descending arrivals in time and handing them to Approach.

Pilot: Center, Lufthansa 4283, flight level three five zero.
CTR: Lufthansa 4283, descend and maintain flight level two four zero.
CTR: Lufthansa 4283, proceed direct Oscar Bravo Kilo, contact Chicago Approach.
  • Start descents about 100-120 NM out (3 NM per 1,000 ft is a good rule).
  • Hand arrivals to Approach below about FL110 and before 40 NM: HO.
  • D OBK sends a plane direct to a radio beacon or airport.

Voice commands

Hold the push-to-talk key (` by default, or the mic button, a mouse side button, a joystick button or the headset button - Settings > Voice) and speak like a controller:

You: "United four five two seven, turn left heading two seven zero, descend and maintain five thousand"
  • Say numbers digit by digit, letters with the alphabet ("Oscar Bravo Kilo").
  • Without a callsign the command goes to the selected plane.
  • The comms log shows what was heard and the command it became. If it misheard, just say it again a bit slower.
  • Settings > Voice > Test microphone checks your microphone.

Practice lessons

Lesson 1 - Your first pushback and taxi

You work: GND only. Open Ground. Push back the first plane, then Taxi. Watch it reach the runway and Tower (AI) take over.

Lesson 2 - Choose the taxi route

Use Route and click the taxiways yourself. Try a route that crosses a runway; press Cross only when the runway is clear.

Lesson 3 - A busy ramp

Handle four departures at once. Use HOLD to stop one plane so another can pass.

Lesson 4 - Tower departures

You work: TWR (Ground on AI). Line up and clear departures with 75 s or more between them.

Lesson 5 - Mixing arrivals and departures

Clear departures between arrivals: only when the next arrival is more than 4 NM out. Clear every arrival to land by 2 NM.

Lesson 6 - Your first vectors

You work: APP. Take one arrival: heading to downwind, descend to 3,000 ft, base turn, intercept heading 30° to final, ILS clearance.

Lesson 7 - Spacing

Two arrivals at once: keep 5 NM between them using speed (S170) and a longer downwind.

Lesson 8 - Center descents

You work: CTR. Descend arrivals early enough to be below FL110 at 40 NM, then hand off (HO).

Lesson 9 - Fixing conflicts

When the red bar appears, read it, decide your own fix before pressing Fix it, then compare.

Lesson 10 - The whole airport

Turn on all four positions. Raise AI flights to 400 and the home share to 25 % in Settings > AI for a real rush.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the landing clearance - the plane goes around. Watch the "6 miles final" calls.
  • Intercepting the localizer at more than 30° - the plane flies through it.
  • Descending too late - arrivals arrive too high for the approach.
  • Crossing a runway with an arrival on short final.
  • Giving too many instructions in one call.
